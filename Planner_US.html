How the model works. In couple mode, both partners contribute the annual 401(k) and IRA limits while employed, subject to earned income; in single-person mode, only Partner A’s limits and income are used. Select Traditional or Roth separately for each account: Traditional contributions reduce modeled taxable wages and fund the traditional account; Roth contributions use after-tax cash flow and fund the Roth account. A separate catch-up slider controls only the age-50+ (and enhanced age-60–63) catch-up portion of the 401(k) limit; catch-up contributions become mandatorily Roth once modeled wages exceed the SECURE 2.0 threshold (~$150k, 2026-indexed), regardless of the slider. Employer 401(k) matches are calculated as the selected percentage of each working person’s salary and added to traditional retirement without reducing take-home pay. Optional bridge/part-time/consulting income (per partner, with its own age window) is taxed as ordinary income with employee-side payroll tax but is not eligible for 401(k)/IRA contributions. Household spending can include up to five sequential changes, each triggered once Partner A reaches a chosen age and fully replacing the prior spending figure from that point on. Up to three additional recurring income sources (each with its own start/end age) are taxed as Partner A's ordinary income and added to cash flow, in today's dollars; up to three one-time lump sums (e.g. an inheritance) are added directly to cash, tax-free, in the year received. Extra mortgage principal accelerates payoff and reduces cash available for brokerage. A "Rent, then buy" housing mode pays rent (with its own growth rate) until a chosen age, then switches to homeownership: the home's future purchase price grows from today's entered value at the home-appreciation rate, and the purchase is funded by cash (down payment plus closing costs, deducted from investable assets in the purchase year) either outright or via a new 15- or 30-year mortgage with its own optional extra-principal payment; from that point on it behaves like Own, including home appreciation, maintenance, mortgage payoff, and home equity in net worth. The emergency reserve stays in HYSA and remaining excess cash flow goes to taxable investments. The five-year bond reserve is built one projected expense-year at a time and split evenly between taxable and traditional accounts. An optional Roth conversion ladder converts traditional balances to Roth each year within a chosen age window, solving for the amount that fills ordinary income up to the top of a selected federal bracket (after any required RMD), automatically reduced further to stay short of the ACA subsidy cliff (once ACA-eligible and not using the enhanced-subsidy assumption) and the next Medicare IRMAA tier (once within two years of 65, matching the real two-year lookback) — whichever constraint is tighter that year. Social Security and pension income offset expenses first; withdrawals then use taxable, traditional, Roth, cash, the protected bond reserve, and finally home equity.
Tax treatment. Choose MFJ or two single filers; an optional marriage age can switch the model from single returns to MFJ. In single years, wages, Social Security, pensions, RMDs, federal/state tax, and NIIT are calculated per person; pooled taxable and traditional withdrawals are split 50/50 for tax purposes. Federal taxable Social Security is recalculated using provisional income after modeled brokerage gains, traditional withdrawals, and any Roth conversion. State calculations receive wages, pension income, traditional/RMD withdrawals, taxable Social Security, and brokerage gains as separate sources. The model applies the broad retirement-income exclusions that its inputs can identify—for example, full exemptions in Illinois and Pennsylvania; age-based exemptions in Iowa and Mississippi; and limited exclusions in Arkansas, New York, South Carolina, and Georgia—alongside state Social Security treatment. This is still a planning estimate: local income tax, state credits, itemizing, military/government-pension distinctions, after-tax IRA basis, special capital-gain treatment, and state-specific phaseouts are not fully modeled. Traditional funds are assumed split 50/50 for RMD purposes, and each partner's RMD begins at their own age 75 using approximate Uniform Lifetime divisors. Brokerage withdrawals use the selected cost-basis ratio to estimate the long-term-gain portion. Taxable interest and dividends are not separately modeled. Traditional IRA deductibility and Roth IRA eligibility limits are not modeled.
Healthcare, ACA & IRMAA. While a partner is retired and under 65, the "annual post-retirement healthcare" figure is treated as the full benchmark premium; if the ACA subsidy toggle is on, it's netted against a modeled premium tax credit using household MAGI (wages, pension, RMDs, Social Security, and any Roth conversion realized that year — modeled brokerage capital gains are not included in this MAGI estimate) and household size (1 for single-person, 2 for couples; dependents aren't modeled). Current 2026 law reverts to the pre-2021 structure: a sliding subsidy from 100%–400% of the federal poverty line (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-25 applicable percentages), then a hard cliff above 400% with no credit at all; a checkbox instead models the 2021–2025 enhanced structure (no cliff, capped at 8.5% of income) in case Congress extends it. From age 65, healthcare cost uses the flat figure plus Medicare IRMAA — the 2026 Part B + Part D surcharge tiers, applied per Medicare-enrolled partner against household MAGI from two simulated years prior, matching Medicare's actual two-year lookback. Neither ACA nor IRMAA account for the Medicaid coverage gap, state ACA variations, or IRMAA appeals for life-changing events.
Historical S&P 500 backtest. The "Historical (S&P 500)" chart view replays your exact inputs starting from every calendar year 1928–2025 with a full run of actual data available for your projection length (calendar-year S&P 500 total returns with dividends reinvested, CPI inflation, and 10-year US Treasury bond total returns, all from the Damodaran/NYU Stern dataset), rather than a single assumed return. It uses a cash-and-bond "bucket" strategy instead of the standard waterfall: the bond pool is sized to your chosen years of expenses; in a year the S&P 500 fell, that year's spending comes from cash then the bond pool only, so equities are never sold at a loss; in a year the market rose, spending is paid from taxable → traditional → Roth and any bond-pool shortfall is refilled the same way. If cash and bonds are both exhausted during a losing stretch, the model falls back to taxable → traditional → Roth → home equity to avoid a false failure. Bonds now grow at the same year's actual 10-year Treasury bond return, so the bond pool's refill/protection value reflects real historical bond performance, not a flat assumption. HYSA and mortgage rates still use your entered assumptions, since no historical series is loaded for either. Every other feature (taxes, RMDs, ACA, IRMAA, the Roth ladder, bridge income, spending tiers, the rent-then-buy housing switch) runs exactly as in the standard projection for each historical year replayed. "Success" means the portfolio never fully depletes by the end of your projection in that starting year; it says nothing about calendar years after 2025, which aren't in the data.
