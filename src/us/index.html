    What the projection calculates from your inputs, and what it leaves out. The results are projections, not advice.

    Limitations & disclaimer. Planning estimate only, not tax, investment or legal advice. This tool was built with AI assistance (Claude) and may contain mistakes; verify the figures independently before making financial decisions. What the model leaves out is listed at the end of this card.

    Contributions and income

    • While working, each partner contributes a chosen share of the 401(k) and IRA limits, Traditional or Roth per account, including the age-50 and age-60–63 catch-ups (made Roth above the SECURE 2.0 wage threshold). Employer match and profit sharing count within the compensation and annual-additions limits; an optional mega backdoor Roth uses the rest.
    • The traditional IRA deduction phases out for a partner covered by a workplace plan. The nondeductible part is tracked as basis (taxed pro rata on later withdrawals), and an optional backdoor Roth converts it each year.
    • Social Security and a pension start at the ages you enter, at the amounts you enter.
    • Up to 10 income streams, each with an owner, an age window, a growth rate and a kind: not earned (ordinary income), W-2 wages (payroll tax; can make plan contributions), or self-employment (self-employment tax, the QBI deduction, a Solo 401(k) and the self-employed health deduction).
    • Up to 10 one-time sums: tax-free cash; taxable wages, ordinary income or a long-term gain; or an inherited traditional or Roth IRA, drawn under the 10-year rule (over the heir's life expectancy for a death before 2020).

    Spending and housing

    • Base spending, plus up to 10 spending changes, each with a start and end age and its own growth; where changes overlap, the one that started latest applies. Recurring extra expenses, one-time or repeating expenses and other loans (10 each) are added on top. Costs for children are not added automatically.
    • Own, rent, or rent then buy, with property tax, insurance, maintenance, extra principal and home appreciation.
    • Optional Guyton-Klinger guardrails: in retirement, when the withdrawal rate drifts more than 20% from its starting rate, spending is cut or raised 10%, staying within 0.5× to 1.5× of plan.

    Saving and withdrawals

    • A surplus fills the cash reserve, then the bond reserve, then the taxable brokerage account. Brokerage cost basis starts from the ratio you enter and is tracked from there; dividends and interest are taxed every year.
    • The bond reserve holds the years of expenses you choose (0–15). It is built over the years before retirement (traditional first, then Roth, taxable last) and kept at that size afterwards: a surplus refills it, and a shortfall is refilled from stocks, at most one year of expenses a year.
    • Default order: taxable, traditional, Roth, cash, the bond reserve, home equity. “Smooth withdrawals” splits the draw between taxable and traditional instead. While anyone would owe the 10% early-withdrawal penalty, penalty-free money is used first, then cash, the bond reserve and Roth contributions, then penalized money, cheapest first. 72(t) payments (per account) and the Rule of 55 (per partner) make money penalty-free.
    • Each partner's RMDs come from their own accounts, from age 73 or 75 by birth year, on the IRS Uniform Lifetime Table. Inherited IRAs pay their own yearly minimum.

    Roth conversions and the cliff plan

    • The optional conversion ladder fills income to the top of a chosen federal bracket each year in its age window, after RMDs and the year's own withdrawals, held under the ACA 400% line while a partner buys marketplace coverage and under the next IRMAA tier for a retired partner from 63.
    • The cliff plan (on by default) picks years to keep income under an ACA, Medicaid or IRMAA line, or to fill above one. With conversions on, it also compares other brackets and per-year conversion sizes, including none; with “Tax-aware withdrawal order” on, it can also draw traditional money before taxable in chosen years and, on separate returns, pick whose accounts go first; a choice is kept only when the projection does not run out sooner. The plan optimizer searches whole plans for an objective you pick, and you can apply the plan it finds.

    Taxes

    • 2026 federal law, indexed for inflation in nominal views. The filing status follows your inputs: married filing jointly or two single filers, switching at an optional marriage age, with head of household and qualifying surviving spouse applied automatically.
    • The larger of the standard deduction (with the 65+ add-ons) and a simplified itemized total (state and property tax under the SALT cap, mortgage interest, medical costs over 7.5% of income), plus the 2025–2028 senior deduction. Taxable Social Security from provisional income, long-term gain rates on gains and qualified dividends, NIIT, payroll tax with the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax, and the child tax credit and credit for other dependents.
    • On separate returns, traditional income follows each partner's own accounts; brokerage gains are split 50/50.

    Healthcare

    • The pre-65 premium applies to a partner without employer coverage (by default a retired partner stays on a working spouse's plan; a checkbox turns that off). With the ACA subsidy on, the premium credit uses the same year's MAGI, solved until withdrawals and the subsidy agree, with qualifying children counted in the household size. 2026 law has a hard cliff at 400% of the poverty line; a checkbox models the 2021–2025 enhanced subsidy instead.
    • Medicaid: $0 premium below 138% of the poverty line in expansion states (when you choose to enroll), no credit below 100% in non-expansion states, and, from 2027, the work requirement (an adult without a child 13 or younger is assumed not to meet it and pays the full premium).
    • Medicare starts at 65, or when a job held past 65 ends. IRMAA uses Medicare's MAGI from two years earlier (for the first two years, your entries if you give them, else the first projected year's), with the life-changing-event appeal (form SSA-44) after a retirement, a job ending or a spouse's death.
    • Healthcare costs grow with inflation plus the extra healthcare growth you set; optional long-term-care costs per partner.

    When a partner dies

    • From the death age: a joint return that year, then single (or qualifying surviving spouse or head of household); the larger Social Security benefit continues, the deceased's pension and healthcare end, spending drops to the survivor share you set, and other loans are paid off.

    Historical and Monte Carlo views

    • Historical: your plan replayed from every start year 1928–2025 with enough data for its length (S&P 500 and 10-year Treasury total returns from the Damodaran/NYU Stern dataset, December-to-December CPI). It uses a bucket strategy: in a down year spending comes from cash, then bonds; in an up year from stocks, with the bond reserve refilled at most one year of expenses a year. The cliff plan and conversion sizes are planned once on your assumptions and replayed. Cash and mortgage rates stay at your inputs. A start year succeeds when every year's spending is met to the end.
    • Monte Carlo: paths built from random 5-year blocks of the same history, seeded from your inputs so the same plan gives the same result, with the same bucket strategy.

    Not modeled

    • Roth IRA income limits, married filing separately, charitable gifts and QCDs, short-term capital gains.
    • Social Security claiming-age adjustments, the earnings test and the full survivor rules (the larger benefit continues).
    • Pension survivor benefits and pensions without inflation indexing.
    • The Medicare late-enrollment penalty, and IRMAA appeals for other life events (marriage, divorce, a lost pension).
    • State itemized deductions, earned-income and circuit-breaker credits, local income tax outside Maryland and Indiana.
    • S corporations and business losses.
    • Costs of selling or borrowing against the home when home equity is used.

Sources: IRS 2026 brackets, limits and capital-gains thresholds, IRS RMD tables, each state's own tax authority (named in the planner's state rules), CMS 2026 Medicare IRMAA tiers, IRS premium tax credit rules, and the Damodaran/NYU Stern historical S&P 500 & inflation dataset.

    Retirement accounts

    Please set your Roth basis. Basis is what you actually contributed (not earnings). It can be withdrawn at any age with no tax or penalty, so it decides how much Roth money an early retiree can reach before 59½. Without it the planner assumes half of each Roth balance is basis. Enter it under Retirement accounts in the sidebar (Roth IRA basis in dollars, Roth 401(k) as a percent).
