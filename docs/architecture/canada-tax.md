# Canada tax

Read before touching `federalTaxCA`, `personTaxCA`, `provincialTaxCA`, `incomeTaxCA`, `PROVINCE_RULES`, `solvePensionSplit`, `payrollItems` or the after-tax estate.

Related: [canada-benefits.md](canada-benefits.md), [engine.md](engine.md), [test-inventory.md](test-inventory.md), [lists.md](lists.md).

## Canada tax

- **Canada federal brackets.** `FED_CAPS` / `FED_RATES` (`core/tax/rules.js`) are the 2026 CRA brackets, 14 / 20.5 / 26 / 29 / 33% (CRA T4127, 122nd ed., Table 8.1: R = 0.2900 for the fourth; it was 0.2929 until 2026-10-23). The RRSP meltdown bracket choice (`#meltdownBracket`, `federalBracketCeilingCA`) uses the same percentages as its keys, so a saved `meltdownBracket: 29.29` is migrated to 29 in `normalizeState` (otherwise it matches no row and falls back to the 33% ceiling).
- **Canada federal credits (7J).** `federalTaxCA(ctx, income, yearIndex, extraCreditBase)` takes the basic personal amount as a 14% credit (`FED_CREDIT_RATE`; $16,452 phasing to $14,829 over `FED_BPA_PHASE_START`-`FED_BPA_PHASE_END`, keyed on net income), never as a deduction at the marginal rate, floored at zero. `personTaxCA(ctx, ordinary, gains, yearIndex, dtcBase, age, pensionIncome)` computes the age amount (65+, 15% clawback over the threshold, both indexed) and the pension income amount ($2,000, **not indexed**) in `ageAndPensionCreditBase` and passes the sum as `extraCreditBase`; Quebec's 0.835 abatement applies after them. Per-person data comes from `buildTaxProfile` (`ageA/B`, `penA/B` = eligible pension income after the split transfer; from `RRIF_PENSION_AGE` = 71 `taxFor` adds the discretionary RRSP/RRIF draw as RRIF income: an RRSP withdrawal is not pension income before the RRIF conversion, 7K); a profile without them reads as under 65. `solvePensionSplit(ctx, ..., ageA, ageB)` sees the credits and, with a 65+ partner, scans a 48-cell grid, refines inside the best cell and also tries the equal-income transfer (combined tax is non-convex through the clawback; under 65 it is the original ternary search). Brackets apply to income itself, so `solveMeltdown` fills to the bracket cap with no BPA term.
- **OAS recovery tax (Canada, 7N N1).** `personTaxCA(ctx, ..., partnerPool, oas)` takes the OAS the person received and charges the recovery tax on the return's full net income: 15% over `OAS_RECOVERY_THRESHOLD` (95,323, indexed), up to the OAS. As on the T1 the repayment is deducted from net income first (line 23500, so the brackets and the age amount see the lower figure) and added to the tax payable (line 42200); credits do not reduce it and Quebec's abatement does not touch it. `incomeTaxCA` passes `profile.oasA/oasB` (already on the profile since 7L; the survivor remap puts the survivor's OAS in slot A) and leaves each return's recovery in the module scratch `OAS_RECOVERY.a/.b` (written only when a return has OAS; read it right after the `taxFor` call whose income you want). Because Canada `taxFor` is the net cost, every draw solver prices the 15% with no solver change. `solvePensionSplit(ctx, ..., ageA, ageB, oasA, oasB)` carries the OAS so the kink is inside its objective. In the year loop the cash flow starts from GROSS OAS and `tax` includes the recovery; the row keeps `ss` (CPP plus OAS, a field name inherited from the US planner) net of the recovery, adds `oasClawback`, and reports `tax` WITHOUT the recovery (as `benefits` is added back), so tables and the Sankey show each amount once. Tests: `tests/oas_recovery.js` (engine), the OAS section of `tests/unit/canada/tax-brackets.test.js`, the high-income scenario of `tests/survivor_symmetry.js`. `tests/bench_canada.js --base DIR` is the Canada timing gate (interleaved, one process).
- **After-tax estate (Canada, 7N N8; display only).** `runProjection` attaches the final balances as a NON-ENUMERABLE `estate` property (`traditional`, `taxable`, `taxableBasis`, `roth`, `cash`, `homeEquity`), so the result's own fields and every snapshot are unchanged. `afterTaxEstate(ctx, sim)` (the context of the run that made `sim`; `computeRothOptimizer` and the printed report pass Canada's `storeContext()`) = last row's total less the tax the RRSP/RRIF and the deemed gains (50% inclusion) add to one final return (stacked on the last survivor's share of the last year's income, via `personTaxCA`) less `probateFee(rule, taxable + cash + home equity)`; registered accounts and the TFSA are assumed to pass by beneficiary designation (outside probate). Row family `probate` per province (`steps` flat fees up to a limit, `base` the fee above the last step, `bands` marginal rates per $1,000 or part: Ontario 15 over 50,000, BC 6 and 14, Alberta steps to 525, Saskatchewan 7, Manitoba and Quebec none, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI, Newfoundland and Labrador per their statutes). Shown beside End assets in the printed report; `computeRothOptimizer` ranks by the highest after-tax estate among the plans that do not shorten the portfolio (it used to minimize lifetime tax; lifetime tax stays in the table); `renderRothOptimizer` is SHARED code (`src/shared/ui/results/roth-optimizer-card.js`, one text for both planners), so the Canada-only `annotateRothOptimizer`, called right after it from `render()` and fed by `lastRothOptimizer`, rewrites the summary and the five-column rows. Tests: `tests/unit/canada/estate.test.js` (Vitest, 27 checks, in `npm test`) and `tests/estate_optimizer.js` (Playwright, the 5 optimizer-ranking checks that need the ui/ `computeRothOptimizer`, in `npm test`).
- **Quebec health contributions (Canada, 7N N7).** Row family `healthContributions` (Quebec only). `incomeTaxCA` (Quebec only) adds, per return, the Health Services Fund contribution `quebecHsf(non-employment income)` (net income less wages, OAS and the dividend gross-up; 1% from the first threshold to 150, flat, then 1% from the second to 1,000; caps unindexed) and the RAMQ premium `quebecRamq(ctx, family income, couple, kids, yearIndex)` for each partner with no wages this year (every retired adult; owner decision), and adds the premium to the medical expenses. The premium table is the 2025 Schedule K scaled by 777.48 / 755 to the 2026 calendar maximum; the income at which it reaches the maximum is cached (`RAMQ_CAP`) so a household above it costs one comparison. Cost: not per call but call volume (the extra 1,900 of yearly need pushes more years into two-account draws); see the 7N notes. Tests: `tests/quebec_health.js`.
- **Low-income provincial tax reductions (Canada, 7N N6).** Row family `lowIncomeReduction` (null in AB, SK, MB and QC, whose forms have none), figures from the CRA 2025 provincial forms (British Columbia: the 2026 T4127 figures). `kind: 'perReturn'` (Ontario: `multiplier x (basic + child x redKids)` less the tax, off the tax) and `kind: 'income'` (British Columbia: `basic - rate x (net income - base)`) run inside `provincialTaxCA` after the dividend credit and before Ontario's health premium (`redKids` = children born in the last 18 years, claimed by the higher-income partner); `kind: 'family'` (NB, NL, NS, PE) is one household reduction worked out in `incomeTaxCA` by `lowIncomeFamilyReduction` (basic, spouse or eligible dependant, children, PEI age amounts, less a rate on adjusted family income over a base, limited to the sum of both returns' provincial tax, which each `provincialTaxCA` call leaves in the scratch `PROV_TAX.last`). Nova Scotia's 1,000 age tax credit is per return. Amounts index with the BPA flag. `solvePensionSplit` mirrors Ontario's children through `pay.rk`; the family reduction is not in its objective. Not built: Ontario's dependants with an impairment and the LIFT credit (Schedule ON428-A). Tests: the N6 block of `tests/unit/canada/tax-brackets.test.js`.
- **Medical expense credit (Canada, 7N N5).** `profile.medical` is the year's eligible expenses: the private health premium plus the WHOLE LTC cost (`healthcareCost` now runs before `buildTaxProfile`; owner decision: nursing-home care). One claim a household: `medicalClaimToB(ctx, netA, netB, yearIndex)` names the preferred claimant (the lower net income unless that partner is under the basic personal amount), and `incomeTaxCA` and `solvePensionSplit` price the other partner too, keeping the cheaper, only when the preferred return had no federal or provincial tax left for the credit (read from `PROV_TAX.fed` / `PROV_TAX.pre`, written by every `personTaxCA` call) and the other return has some; in Ontario with a 70+ spouse both claimants are always priced on tax less the Seniors Care at Home credit, whose floor depends on the claimant (keep that test a plain `ctx.inputs.province` comparison: reading the rule row in this hot function measured 1.2 to 1.35x slower in every province) (a 65+ partner owes nothing up to about 27,700, so the preferred claim could be wasted; 7N review fix; the low-income reduction and surtax interplay is not priced). `personTaxCA(ctx, ..., provDeduction, medical)` adds `max(0, medical - min(3% of net income, FED_MEDICAL_THRESHOLD x factor))` to the federal credit base; `provincialTaxCA(ctx, ..., extraCredit, medical)` does the same from the row family `medical: { threshold, familyIncome }` (Quebec: no dollar floor, 3% of family income). Ontario's refundable Seniors Care at Home credit (`careAtHome` row family; `careAtHomeCredit`) is subtracted in `incomeTaxCA` (net tax can be negative). `solvePensionSplit(ctx, ..., kids, pay)` mirrors the claimant through `pay.med`. Not built: the federal top-up tax credit, Quebec's refundable medical credit. Tests: the N5 block of `tests/unit/canada/tax-brackets.test.js`, `tests/medical_credit.js`.
- **Payroll items and the home buyers' amount (Canada, 7N N4 and N10).** `payrollItems(ctx, wages, yearIndex)` fills the scratch object `PAY` (read it right after the call; the test hook returns a copy): `cash` (CPP or QPP, CPP2, EI at `payroll.eiRate`, QPIP), `fedCredit` (base CPP/QPP = `pensionRate` less the 1% first additional, plus EI, QPIP and the $1,501 Canada employment amount), `provCredit` (the same without the employment amount; zero in Quebec, which gives no credit for them), `deduction` (the enhanced CPP/QPP: 1% on the band plus the 4% second tier; a deduction from income federally, provincially and in Quebec) and `provDeduction` (Quebec's workers deduction, 6% to $1,450, provincial only). It is computed once a year in `buildTaxProfile` (never inside the solvers): the deduction lowers `otherA/B`, the credit bases ride on the profile as `fedCreditA/B`, `provCreditA/B`, `provDedA/B` (the survivor remap moves B's into slot A) and `personTaxCA(ctx, ..., childKids, fedCredit, provCredit, provDeduction)` applies them; `solvePensionSplit(ctx, ..., kids, pay)` takes the same numbers as one object so the split sees them. `payrollTax` returns `cash` for both earners (the cash cost reaches the engine through it). The home buyers' amount is a flat non-refundable credit base in the `rentThenBuy` purchase year (`housingYear.homeValue !== null`): the federal 10,000 and the row's `homeBuyer.amount` (Saskatchewan 15,000, Quebec 10,000; every other province none), on the higher-income partner's return. Not built: Quebec's tax credit for access to homeownership (up to 5,875), the EI/CPP effects of a person who works past 70. Tests: the N4/N10 block of `tests/unit/canada/tax-brackets.test.js`, `tests/payroll_credits.js` (engine, including the purchase-year plumbing).
- **Spouse, eligible-dependant and child amounts (Canada, 7N N3).** Federal: `federalTaxCA(ctx, income, yearIndex, extraCreditBase, claimMinus)` adds `max(0, bpa - claimMinus)` to the credit base, where `bpa` is the claimant's own phased basic personal amount (16,452 to 14,829) and `claimMinus` is the spouse's net income (0 for a dependant child; -1 means no claim). Provincial: each `PROVINCE_RULES` row has `spouse: { amount, threshold }` (CRA TD1 forms 2026; `null` for Quebec) and `childAmount` (Saskatchewan only: 8,358 for each child under 18 besides the one claimed as the eligible dependant); the credit amount is `clamp(amount + threshold - spouse net income, 0, amount)` and indexes with the BPA flag (Manitoba frozen). Quebec has no spouse amount, and its additional amount for a person living alone (single-parent family; 2,681 for 2026) is NOT built: the primary rule (Revenu Quebec guide line 361) gives it only to someone living with a child 18 or over in post-secondary studies who is not entitled to the Family Allowance, so a parent of a minor child gets nothing (the living-alone amount already covers a single parent). `personTaxCA(ctx, ..., oas, spouseNet, depKids, childKids)`: `spouseNet >= 0` is a spouse claim, `depKids > 0` a single claimant's eligible dependant (one child; the rest are child-amount children), `childKids` the children whose child amount this return claims. `incomeTaxCA` decides who claims: the higher-income partner claims the spouse amount (A on a tie) and the lower-income parent the children (A on a tie); a single person or survivor claims the dependant. `solvePensionSplit(ctx, ..., oasA, oasB, kids)` mirrors the same rule on the post-transfer incomes (a test that re-prices the split must mirror it too: `claimNets` in `tests/unit/canada/tax-brackets.test.js`). Children under 18 come from `profile.dependents` (`countKids(deps, 0, 17)`). Not built: the transfer of a spouse's unused amounts. Tests: the N3 block of `tests/unit/canada/tax-brackets.test.js`, `tests/dependant_amounts.js` (engine), the child scenarios of `tests/survivor_symmetry.js`.
- **Provincial tax (Canada, `PROVINCE_RULES`, 7K).** One data row per province (the 10; no territories) between the `// PROVINCE_RULES:begin` / `end` comments (pure literals, read by `scripts/gen-province-rules.js`, which writes `docs/province-tax-rules.md`; `npm run gen:provinces`; `npm test` fails if it is stale and runs `tests/unit/canada/province-rules.test.js`, the coverage test). A row declares every field: `creditRate` (the lowest bracket rate: every non-refundable amount is worth that), `brackets`, `bpa` (amount, optional `phase` down to a floor: Manitoba only in 2026), `age` (amount, threshold, 15% reduction rate, optional unreduced `supplement`: Saskatchewan), `pension` (the provincial maximum), `pooled` (Quebec: age, retirement-income (the lesser of $3,541 and 1.25 times the pension or RRIF income) and living-alone amounts, reduced together by 18.75% of family income over $42,955; a couple's two pools are added and reduced once, each spouse taking a share in proportion to their own amounts: `quebecPoolAmount` and the `partnerPool` argument), `payroll` (CPP or QPP rate and Quebec's QPIP premium; `cppContribution` reads it), `dividend.eligible`, Ontario's `surtax` and `healthPremium`, `indexing` (one boolean per family: Manitoba's schedule is frozen; AB, ON and NS pension amounts are indexed, the flat $1,000 ones are not) and `verified` (primary or secondary per family). `compileProvince` splits each row once per code; `provincialTaxCA(ctx, income, yearIndex, dtcBase, age, pensionIncome, familyIncome, partnerPool, spouseNet, depKids, childKids)` allocates nothing: tax on the brackets, less credits at `creditRate` (floored at zero), then Ontario's surtax on that, then the eligible dividend credit against provincial tax only (federal and provincial dividend credits are floored separately; Ontario's surtax comes before its dividend credit, as on form ON428), then the Health Premium (on taxable income, not reduced by credits). `familyIncome` is read only by Quebec: a number for a couple (built in `taxFor` and `solvePensionSplit`), `null` for a single filer or survivor (own income, lives alone). Quebec's 16.5% abatement applies after the federal credits, the dividend credit included. **Not modeled:** a spouse's transfer of unused amounts, non-eligible Canadian dividends (taxed like interest), the Ontario LIFT credit (low-income tax reductions are built in 7N N6 for ON, BC, NB, NL, NS and PE; AB, SK, MB and QC have none). The paid benefits and the Ontario property-tax/rent credit are modeled in 7L (`BENEFIT_RULES`, below). To add a family, add the field to every row, the coverage test and the generator. Verification status and the backlog are in the 7K notes in `docs/next-batches-plan.md`.
