## Income-tested benefits

- **Income-tested benefits (Canada, `BENEFIT_RULES`, 7L).** One pure-literal data row per program between the `// BENEFIT_RULES:begin` / `end` comments (read by `scripts/gen-benefit-rules.js`, which writes `docs/benefits-rules.md`; `npm run gen:benefits`; `npm test` fails if it is stale and runs `tests/unit/canada/benefit-rules.test.js`, the coverage test). Row kinds: `gisAllowance` (the six ESDC household situations as `[income, annual benefit]` knots fitted to tables 1 to 5, interpolated by `interpKnots` / `gisAnnual`, scaled with the plan inflation factor), `oasPension` (the 75-plus factor 1.10 and the recommended 2026 amount: **update for 2027**, quarterly-indexed), `familyCredit` (CGEB, Ontario Sales Tax Credit), `childSchedule` (CCB, Ontario Child Benefit, BC Family Benefit, ACFB, Quebec Family Allowance), `seniorSupplement` (Alberta Seniors Benefit), `occupancyCredit` (from rent or property tax: Ontario Energy and Property Tax Credit and senior homeowners grant; 7M adds the New Brunswick property tax allowance, the Nova Scotia seniors property tax rebate, which reads `BEN.gis` and `BEN.allowance`, and the Manitoba homeowners credit, seniors school tax rebate and renters credit, the Manitoba figures are those of the 2025 Form MB479 and scale with plan inflation, the renters credit is limited by the rent paid and the seniors rebate by the tax left after the homeowners credit, the others are unindexed; the coverage test allows scope `on`, `mb`, `ns`, `nb`; the Manitoba homeowners credit proxies school taxes by the whole property tax) and `quebecCredit` (solidarity credit, work premium). Each row has `source`, `asOf`, `retrieved`, `verified` per family (primary or secondary), `indexing` and `dependsOn`. `benefitsFor(ctx, profile, gains, traditionalExtra)` fills the module-level scratch object `BEN` (read with `getBenefits()`; the next call overwrites it), allocation-free, in a fixed order (GIS and Allowance first, then the child and credit programs by `ctx.inputs.province`), with an early exit for a household with no children above 125,000 (skipped for Manitoba, whose homeowners and renters credits have no income test). **Net-tax design:** Canada `taxFor` is `incomeTaxCA(...) - benefitsFor(...)` (the benefits are tax-free cash, so net cash is identical to a separate inflow), which lets the shared `drawTaxable` and the Canada solvers price lost benefits as a cost of withdrawing without editing the shared `drawTaxable` (`src/shared/core/withdrawals/draws.js`); rows keep `tax` gross and add `benefits` plus one field per line (`benefitGis`, `benefitAllowance`, `benefitCcb`, `benefitCgeb`, `benefitProvChild`, `benefitProvSenior`, `benefitProvCredit`). Profile fields for it: `oasA/B`, `earnedA/B`, `survivor`, `dependents` (from `dependentsInYear`), `rentPaid`, `propertyTaxPaid` (from `housingForYear`). Same-year income stands in for the prior-year return (an engine convention). `gisIncomeRoom` is the hook the Canada mixed-withdrawal batch (4A) reads. Step cliffs (7M): the New Brunswick allowance tiers and the Nova Scotia rebate (which stops when GIS stops, up to 800) drop in a step, which used to leave the net at the top of the `drawTaxable` bracket (`need * 3 + 1`) below the need and return a negative net (measured: Nova Scotia, single 70, property tax 3,000, other income 32,212, need 100: net -563). `drawTaxable` and the two Canada RRSP solvers (`solveTraditionalForNet`, `solveTraditionalForNetFromNeed`) now widen the bracket (doubling, at most 12 times, up to the pool) only when the net at its top is short of the need, so every draw that did not need it is bit-identical (the snapshots did not move) and the extra cost is one tax evaluation per call (measured +3 to +7 percent, ten percent noise). At a cliff the net is not monotone, so the result is a valid draw, not necessarily the smallest sale. Test: `tests/unit/canada/benefits.test.js`, "step cliffs and the withdrawal solvers". Known approximation: where a benefit phases IN as income rises (the CGEB single supplement at 2 percent, later the ACFB and work premium), the net of a taxable sale can exceed the gross, and the shared `drawTaxable` bisection (which starts at the amount needed) over-sells by up to that phase-in amount. 7M completes the ten provinces: the child benefits, family and low-income credits and seniors programs of SK (Low-Income Tax Credit, Income Plan), MB (Child Benefit, 55 PLUS junior component), NS (Child Benefit, Affordable Living Tax Credit), NB (Child Tax Benefit, Working Income Supplement, School Supplement, HST credit, Low-Income Seniors Benefit), NL (Child Benefit, Income Supplement, Seniors Benefit) and PEI (Child Benefit, Island Essentials Benefit) are `childSchedule`, `familyCredit` and `seniorSupplement` rows with a `case` each in `benefitsFor`. Helpers: `countKids(kids, minAge, maxAge)` and `familyCreditCA(R, f, fam, couple, n)` (the CGEB shape: adult, spouse or eligible dependant, per child with an optional `childCap`, reduced over a threshold; `indexing.amountsAndThresholds` decides whether `f` applies). `BEN_NO_EXIT` lists the provinces whose programs pay at any income, which skip the 125,000 early exit: Manitoba (the homeowners and renters credits; the Child Benefit and 55 PLUS are income-tested) and PEI (the Island Essentials Benefit pays at least 175 at any income). Several shapes are readings and are marked secondary in their rows: the Saskatchewan Seniors Income Plan follows its regulation (RRS c S-25.1 Reg 3 s. 5(2), read from the consolidation through SR 39/2023): the maximum falls by 1.90, 2.10 or 6.55 dollars per dollar of base GIS reduction, which is a straight line from the maximum at no income to the published nil point for a single person, a couple and a spouse on the Allowance (190 bands of 24 and 155 bands of 48 reproduce 4,560 and 7,440), and flat at the maximum to income 9,184 then to the published 11,568 for a pensioner whose spouse is not a pensioner (the regulation formula alone gives about 11,822) and the Manitoba Child Benefit is a straight line between the published full-benefit income and the published nil point, the Saskatchewan credit rate is derived from the published maximum and nil income, the Manitoba 55 PLUS senior component (Regulation 65/90 tables by household type) and the NL Nutrition Supplement (150 a month a child under five, with the Child Benefit) are built from their primary pages, the NL Income Supplement phases IN with family income between 15,000 and 20,000 (the known `drawTaxable` phase-in limitation; the NB Working Income Supplement phases in on earned income, which a withdrawal does not change), the Manitoba 55 PLUS junior component is not paid to someone who receives the Allowance (an OAS Act benefit), and the Saskatchewan single-parent amount (eligible dependant plus child amounts) is a reading. Not built: the CSV export has no benefit columns (the export is shared code, `exportCsv` in `src/shared/ui/plans/export-and-print.js`); the NL pregnancy and birth parts of the Nutrition Supplement; the PEI provincial property tax credit (needs the assessed value), Manitoba Rent Assist and the Nova Scotia heating rebate (inputs we do not have); disability benefits; low-income tax reductions; the prior-year lookback. Tests: `tests/unit/canada/benefit-rules.test.js`, `tests/unit/canada/gis-tables.test.js` (every row of the ESDC tables, from `docs/sources/gis-tables-2026q4.json`), `tests/unit/canada/benefits.test.js` (hand-computed cases citing the page or statute).
