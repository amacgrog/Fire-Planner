# Feedback B3: revolving debt (credit cards and lines of credit) in the loans list (plan, 2026-11-16)

Item B3 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-first-user-feedback-plan.md`. The first user's note #11 (debt is a big factor) and
#2 (people budget the minimum card payment, so projections look rosier than they are); the owner found that a balance with no
fixed term cannot be entered: every loan row is amortized from an original principal and term. Both planners (the loans list,
its normalizer and `loanSchedules()` are shared). One PR against `main` (B2, PR #102, merged at af3247c). Badges: US V181 →
V182, Canada V85 → V86.

## The owner's decision (2026-11-16)

Each month's payment is the **larger** of (a) a fixed monthly amount the user enters and (b) the card's minimum: a percent of
the balance with a dollar floor (for example 2% or $25), never more than the balance. A fixed amount of 0 pays the minimum
only. Recorded in the spec's B3 entry in this PR.

## The design

### The month (engine)

In `src/shared/core/income/spending-and-loans.js`, a pure helper and its use in `loanSchedules`:

```js
  // A revolving row (a credit card or a line of credit; feedback B3), month by month for `months` months from today. Each month
  // the balance accrues a twelfth of the APR, then the payment is the larger of the fixed amount and the card's minimum (the
  // percent of that statement balance, at least the floor), never more than the balance. One APR for the whole run; no new
  // charges (they are spending). Returns the yearly schedule and what the row's note shows.
  export function revolvingSchedule(loan, months) { ... }
```

Each month while the balance is above 0:

```
interest = balance * rate            // rate = pct(apr) / 12
owed     = balance + interest
minimum  = min(owed, max(owed * pct(minPct), minFloor))
pay      = min(owed, max(payment, minimum))
balance  = owed - pay                // exactly 0 on the month pay === owed
```

It returns `{ payments, balances, firstInterest, firstPayment, payoffMonth, totalInterest, fixedFromMonth }`: `payments[y]` /
`balances[y]` for each full year inside `months` (the year's 12 payments; the balance at the year's end), `payoffMonth` the
1-based month the balance reaches 0 (`null` if not inside `months`), `totalInterest` to then, `fixedFromMonth` the first month
the fixed amount sets the payment (`null` with a 0 fixed amount or if never). `loanSchedules` calls it with 71 × 12 months for a
revolving row and keeps only `{ payments, balances }`, the same shape and length as an amortized row, so nothing downstream
changes: the year's payment, the row's loan balance, the death-year payoff (the previous year-end balance, or the row's
balance in year 0), the Sankey item, the CSV and the report read it as they read any loan. An amortized row runs today's code,
untouched.

Not a hot path: `loanSchedules` runs once per projection (and once per Sankey year), not per year of the loop; it allocates
the two arrays an amortized row allocates. It reads only its argument (the row of `ctx.inputs.loans`), so the result caches
key it with the inputs' JSON. No module-level mutable, no snapshot field (`engine-purity.json` and the MAGI rules untouched).

The payment is in nominal dollars, like an amortized payment (no inflation growth). A balance whose payment does not cover the
interest grows for the whole run; with a minimum below the APR's monthly rate that is unbounded growth (a $10,000 card at 22%
and a 1% minimum is about $22.6 million of interest in 71 years, all real). That is the warning's case, and the figures stay
finite.

### Hand-computed figures (exact fractions, Python `fractions.Fraction`, rounded to the cent)

A $10,000 balance at 22% APR, minimum 2% with a $25 floor:

| Fixed payment | Month 1 interest / payment | Year 0 paid / end balance | Year 1 | Payoff | Total interest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $0 (minimum only) | 183.33 / 203.67 | 2,416.85 / 9,758.71 | 2,358.54 / 9,523.24 | month 1,158 (96 years 6 months) | 81,039.69 |
| $400 | 183.33 / 400.00 | 4,800.00 / 7,121.13 | 4,800.00 / 3,540.98 | month 34 (year 2 pays 3,899.76) | 3,499.76 |
| $150 | 183.33 / 203.67 | as $0 | as $0 | month 278 | 35,418.33 |

With $150 the fixed amount first sets the payment in month 152: year 11 pays 1,847.42, year 12 pays 1,807.01 (seven months of
minimum, then five of $150), year 13 pays exactly 1,800.00; year 23 pays 164.57 and ends at 0.

The floor: $1,000 at 22%, 2% / $25, $0 fixed: month 1 interest 18.33, 2% of 1,018.33 is 20.37, so it pays $25; years 0-5 pay
300.00 each (year-end balances 911.42, 801.26, ...; year 5 ends at 18.57); year 6 pays 18.91 and the balance is 0 in month 73.

The warning: $10,000 at 22%, a 1% minimum: month 1 interest 183.33, payment 101.83; year 0 pays 1,278.29 and ends at
11,023.05 (it grows). And at 0% APR (no interest), 2% / $25: year 0 pays 2,152.83, ends at 7,847.17; paid off in month 153.

The figures in the tests are these literals; the engine's are compared rounded to the cent (`Math.round(x * 100) / 100`,
`toBe`), as `loan-schedule.test.js` rounds; the payoff months are integers.

### The row (inputs)

A loan row gains five keys: `type` (`'amortized'` default, `'revolving'`), `minPct` (default 2), `minFloor` (default 25),
`payment` (default 0). `balance` and `rate` are shared by both types (a card's balance today and its APR), so switching the
type keeps them. `principal`, `term` and `extra` show only for an amortized row; `minPct`, `minFloor`, `payment` and the note
only for a revolving row (`showIf`).

- `fresh()` (both `dyn.js`): `{ name: '', type: 'amortized', principal: 0, balance: 0, rate: 0, term: 0, extra: 0, minPct: 2,
  minFloor: 25, payment: 0 }`.
- `normalizeLists`: `type: l.type === 'revolving' ? 'revolving' : 'amortized'` (missing, misspelled or non-string is amortized,
  so an old save loads unchanged); `minPct: num(l.minPct, 2, 0, 100)`, `minFloor: num(l.minFloor, 25, 0)`,
  `payment: num(l.payment, 0, 0)`. The legacy `otherLoansEnabled` filter is unchanged (old saves have no revolving rows).
- The shared revolving field group lives in `src/shared/core/inputs/dyn-fields.js` beside `DYN_GROWTH_FIELDS`, so the two
  countries' tips cannot drift: `DYN_LOAN_TYPE_FIELD` (the select) and `DYN_REVOLVING_FIELDS` (three fields and the note).
  Both `dyn.js` loan specs become: `name`, the type select, `principal` (showIf amortized), `balance`, `rate`, `term` (showIf
  amortized), `extra` (showIf amortized), then `...DYN_REVOLVING_FIELDS`.

The fields (labels; `short`; tip):

- **Type** (select; `[['amortized', 'Fixed-term loan'], ['revolving', 'Credit card or line of credit']]`); tip: "A
  fixed-term loan (car, student, personal) has a set payment from its original amount and term. A credit card or line of
  credit has no term: each month you pay the larger of the payment you choose and the card's minimum."
- **Remaining balance** (unchanged label; money).
- **Interest rate** (num, 0-40, unchanged bounds), new `short` "Yearly %; a card's APR" and tip: "The yearly interest rate,
  in percent; for a card or credit line, its APR (a purchase APR, or a promotional rate only while it lasts). Interest is
  charged monthly at a twelfth of it. One rate for the whole projection: a rate that changes over time is not modeled." The
  tip is on both types (one field), so amortized rows' markup moves (the snapshot moves only where a loan row is drawn).
- **Minimum payment, %** (`minPct`, num, 0-100, step 0.1), short "Of the balance each month; often 1-3%", tip: "Your card's
  minimum payment, as a percent of the month's balance (after that month's interest). Many cards charge 1% of the balance
  plus the month's interest, close to 3% at a 22% APR: enter the total. A minimum below the interest never pays the card
  down."
- **Minimum floor** (`minFloor`, money, step 5), short "The minimum is at least this, e.g. $25".
- **Monthly payment you choose** (`payment`, money, step 25), short "0 pays the minimum only", tip: "Each month you pay the
  larger of this amount and the card's minimum, never more than the balance; 0 pays the minimum only, the slowest and most
  costly way to clear it. The amount stays fixed in dollars (it does not rise with inflation). New charges are not modeled:
  count what you put on the card each month in Annual household spending, and enter here only the balance you carry."
- **The note** (a new `kind: 'note'`, showIf revolving): one line under the fields, from `revolvingLoanNote(item)` (in
  `dyn-fields.js`, pure; it calls `revolvingSchedule(item, 1200)`, 100 years, cheap):
  - balance 0: no text (the note is empty and hidden);
  - payment does not cover the first month's interest (`firstPayment <= firstInterest`, with a balance above 0): class
    `dyn-note warn`, "The first payment ($102) does not cover the month's interest ($183), so the balance grows. Raise the
    payment or the minimum." (0% APR and a $0 payment: "... does not cover the month's interest ($0), so the balance never
    falls", the same test with `<=`; the wording picks "grows" when the interest is above 0, else "never falls");
  - else: "Paid off in 2 years 10 months, with $3,500 in interest." (years and months from `payoffMonth`; "in 8 months" under
    a year; dollars rounded with `money()`); not paid off in 100 years: "Not paid off in 100 years at this payment."

  Rendering: `dynFieldHtml` draws `<p class="dyn-note" data-dyn-wrap="<key>" data-dyn-i data-dyn-k hidden?>` with the text
  through `escapeHtml` (not a `.field`, so field search skips it; not an `input`/`select`, so the edit handlers never match
  it). `dynApplyVisibility` also refreshes each note's text and `warn` class (a note's `showIf` is ANDed with "has text"), and
  each row header (`[data-dyn-title]`, so a type change retitles the row). It runs on every list edit, including typing, as
  the name box's header does: row-local text, no render.

  CSS (`styles.css`): `.dyn-note{font-size:.74rem;color:var(--hint);margin:2px 0 8px}.dyn-note.warn{color:var(--red);font-weight:700}`.

**Row title.** `LIST_ROW_TITLES.loans` becomes `(i, item) => item?.type === 'revolving' ? `Card or credit line ${i + 1}` :
`Loan ${i + 1}``. `listRowLabel` and `itemLabel` pass the item, so the header and the Sankey band of an unnamed card read
"Card or credit line 2"; `rowTitle(i)` without an item (the remove button's label, the undo text) still reads "Loan 2". The
list's `note` becomes "Car loans, personal loans, credit cards and lines of credit — separate from your mortgage."; its `tip`
adds "A card's balance is paid down month by month at the payment you choose or the minimum, whichever is larger."

**Group summary** (both `render-group-summaries.js`): a revolving row adds `revolvingSchedule(l, 12).payments[0]` (its first
year's payments), an amortized row today's `mortgagePayment(...) * 12`, so "✓ 2 loans · $9.6K/yr" counts both.

### The hashes

A new key on every loan row would change `monteCarloSeed` and every saved `storedPlan.madeFor` for each save with a loan, and
the guidance memo key (harmless). New in `lists.js`: `withoutInertListKeys(inputs)` = `withoutListNames(inputs)`, then each
loan row whose `type` is not `'revolving'` drops `type`, `minPct`, `minFloor` and `payment` (the engine reads none of them for
an amortized row). The three call sites switch to it; `withoutListNames` stays (its test). An amortized row then hashes
exactly as before, and a revolving row hashes all its keys (its leftover `principal`/`term`/`extra` too; harmless). A
comment at each site says why.
