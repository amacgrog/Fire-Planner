# Feedback C2: a federal income-tax rate what-if (plan, 2026-11-22)

Item C2 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-first-user-feedback-plan.md`, answering the first real user's note #4 ("it would
be foolhardy to assume current rates and rules stay fixed ... let the user test various federal income tax rates"). US
only; Canada's federal table takes the same switch later. One PR against `main`. Badge: US V198 → V199 (Canada untouched).
The model for this plan is B2 (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-15-feedback-b2.md`): a percent, a start year and a preset
button, applied where the engine computes the thing it changes.

## Owner decisions (2026-11-22)

1. **Which brackets:** every ordinary-income bracket rate is scaled by the same percent, from a chosen calendar year on
   ("federal rates 20% higher from 2030"). Rates only, never the thresholds: the conversion ladder and the cliff plan fill to
   bracket thresholds, so their targets keep their meaning.
2. **Capital gains:** an "Include capital-gains and qualified-dividend rates" box, ticked by default, scales those rates by
   the same percent.
3. **Preset:** one button, "Rates rise 20% from 2030", a plain stress test that models no particular bill. Its values are
   named constants.

Default 0%, so no number moves while it is off.

## The design

### The engine: one scale per call, outside the bracket loop

In `src/us/core/tax/federal-rules.js`, beside `taxYearOf`:

```js
  // Feedback C2: the federal rate what-if. Every ordinary bracket rate (and, with fedRateChangeGains, the 15% and 20% gain
  // rates) is multiplied by 1 + fedRateChangePct / 100 from January of fedRateChangeYear on; the thresholds never move.
  // 1 when off, so x * 1 leaves every figure as it was. The clamp (-50 to +100) keeps every rate under 100%.
  export function fedRateScale(ctx, yearIndex) {
    const p = ctx.inputs.fedRateChangePct;
    return p && taxYearOf(ctx, yearIndex) >= ctx.inputs.fedRateChangeYear ? (100 + p) / 100 : 1;
  }
  export function fedGainRateScale(ctx, yearIndex) {
    return ctx.inputs.fedRateChangeGains === false ? 1 : fedRateScale(ctx, yearIndex);
  }
```

- `ordinaryTax` reads `const s = fedRateScale(ctx, yearIndex)` once, before the loop, and returns `tax * s`. Scaling the sum
  is scaling every rate (each slice's `slice * rate`, summed, times `s`); it is one multiply per call, not one per bracket.
  `(100 + p) / 100` (not `1 + p / 100`), as B2's share, so +20 is the double nearest 1.2.
- `longTermGainTax` returns `(fifteenGain * 0.15 + twentyGain * 0.2) * fedGainRateScale(ctx, yearIndex)`. The 0% band stays
  0%. The bands (the thresholds) do not move.
- Off (the default `fedRateChangePct: 0`): `p` is falsy, the scale is exactly 1, and `x * 1 === x` for every finite double,
  so no row, golden figure or snapshot moves by a bit. An inputs object without the key (a hand-built test profile) reads
  `undefined`, also falsy.
- Hot path: one property read and one test per call while off; while on, one comparison and one division. Nothing is
  allocated, no memo, no module-level mutable: `tests/engine-purity.json` is untouched, and the MAGI-iteration snapshot
  rules do not apply (nothing in the year body is mutated). The per-year factor is read per call exactly as
  `taxIndexFactor` is, never inside the bracket loop. Timed with `tests/bench_runs_interleaved.js` against a worktree of
  `origin/main` (both with the switch off, the default household and `--warm-analysis`); the gate is the usual one, no
  regression outside the run-to-run noise. A second interleaved run with the switch on is reported for information.
- It reads only `ctx.inputs` (the context's own inputs), so `resultKey` and `successKey`, which hash the whole inputs JSON,
  key it with no change: a run with a rate change is never served a cached run without one.

**Every reader sees the same rates as the run**, because they all go through `ordinaryTax` / `longTermGainTax`:
- `taxFor` (all three branches), the lagged federal liability a state that deducts federal tax reads, and the two-filer
  child-credit pass. The nonrefundable child credits are limited to the federal tax, so a higher tax can use more of them:
  correct (the credit amounts are not rates and stay as written).
- `lambdaEstimate`: each sample is priced with `taxFor` under the current plan year's law (`P.year = year`), so it sees the
  scaled rates from the change year on, and a sample of a later year seen from before the change year is priced at today's
  rates. That is the existing rule for every scheduled change (the senior deduction ends after 2028, the SALT cap returns to
  $10,000 in 2030): lambda is an estimate, and the cliff plan keeps a choice only when the whole run, which applies each
  year's own rates, comes out ahead. So a plan that would convert more before a rise finds it through its scored runs, not
  through lambda. Making lambda price each sample at its own year's scale would need the profile's year to differ from the
  indexing year (a new profile field on the hot path); not in C2. Documented in `cliff-plan.md`.
- The plan's ending value (`legacyValue`): priced under the last year's law, so with a rate change on, heirs' tax on the
  traditional balance is at the scaled rates. Correct: the rise is still in force then.
- The bracket-search card, the plan optimizer, the claiming card, the guidance impacts and the verdict all score whole runs
  from `ctx.inputs` (variants are `{ ...state, field }` copies, which carry the three keys).
- The historical backtest and Monte Carlo: the change applies in every view (the tax year advances with the plan year there
  too: `taxYearOf` is `ctx.taxYear + yearIndex` in every mode), as B2's cut does.

### What is not scaled, and why

The user's note is about income-tax rates ("what percent of my gross income I paid ... various federal income tax rates"),
so only the federal income tax's own rate schedule moves. Confirmed against the review's suggestion; each stays at its law:

| Not scaled | Why |
|---|---|
| Payroll tax (Social Security 6.2%, Medicare 1.45%) and self-employment tax (12.4% / 2.9%) | A separate payroll tax with its own statute (FICA, SECA), not the income-tax schedule; a change to Social Security financing is a different what-if (B2's cut is the benefit side) |
| The 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax | Part of the Medicare payroll tax (IRC 3101(b)(2), 1401(b)(2)) |
| The 3.8% net investment income tax | A separate tax (IRC 1411) with its own rate, not a bracket rate; a user who wants it in can raise the gains rates |
| State income tax | The state's law; to compare states, pick another state (state relocation over time is item D1 / E4, not built yet) |
| Thresholds, the standard and senior deductions, the SALT cap, QBI, the child credits, the 0% gain band | Owner decision 1: rates only. These are amounts, not rates, so the conversion targets, the cliff plan's bracket fills and the ACA / IRMAA lines keep their meaning |
| The ACA premium credit, IRMAA, the Medicaid line, the IRA deduction phase-out, the taxable share of Social Security | They read MAGI or provisional income, not the tax, so a rate change cannot move them (the income itself can, through larger withdrawals to pay the tax; that is the model working) |

### The cap

`fedRateChangePct` is clamped to **-50 to +100**. At +100 the top ordinary rate is 74% and the top gain rate 40%; with the
highest state rate (13.3%) and the NIIT (3.8%) a dollar's marginal tax stays under 100%, so the withdrawal gross-up (which
solves a net need) and the conversion solver never meet a marginal rate of 100% or more from the rates themselves. No per-rate
`Math.min(1, rate * s)` is needed: no rate can reach 100% inside the clamp, and a test pins that (the top rate at +100 is
0.74). +100 (doubling every rate) is past any rise enacted since 1980; the 1950s top rate of 91% was on a different schedule.
A negative percent tests a cut (rates 20% lower): allowed down to -50 (the 10% bracket at 5%), since "rates could fall"
is the same question; the descriptor says "+" raises and "-" lowers. A non-number is 0.

### The bracket labels (the conversion bracket select and the bracket-search card)

They keep today's names ("22%"): a bracket is an income range, and the ranges do not move, so "fill to the top of the 22%
bracket" converts the same dollars with or without a rate change; only the tax on them changes. Relabeling to "26.4%" would
make the select's value (the target) depend on the year. The `#rothConvBracket` bubble gains one sentence: "With a federal
rate change set, a bracket keeps its name and its income range: at +20% the 22% bracket is taxed at 26.4% from the change
year." The percent's bubble says "(named by today's rate)". The bracket-search card's intro is unchanged (its rows are
labeled "Fill to bracket").
