## Notes

- **ON**: Surtax of 20% on Ontario tax over $5,818 plus a further 16% over $7,446, computed on the tax after non-refundable credits and before the dividend tax credit (the order on CRA form ON428). The Ontario Health Premium (up to $900) is based on taxable income. Ontario tax reduction is a low-income item (not yet modeled).
- **BC**: The lowest rate is 5.60% for 2026 (BC Ministry of Finance); CRA payroll tables still show 5.06%, a stale payroll figure. No Medical Services Plan premium since 2020.
- **SK**: The senior supplement ($2,569, age 65+, claimable even when the age amount is nil) is added to the age amount and is not reduced by income. Saskatchewan raises its personal amounts by a further $500 a year by statute; modeled as ordinary indexing only.
- **MB**: The basic personal amount, its phase-down (nil from $400,000 net income) and the brackets are frozen, not indexed (CRA T4127). The age amount is also unindexed for 2026 per TaxTips (factor 1.000, secondary).
- **QC**: Quebec files its own provincial return and residents get a 16.5% federal abatement (applied after the federal credits). Its age, living-alone and retirement-income amounts are not modeled yet (own commit).
- **NS**: The old income-tested reduction of the basic personal amount no longer applies in 2026 (CRA T4127: the maximum is available at every income).
- **PE**: The fourth threshold is $142,520 (CRA T4127 and PEI Finance; the code had $142,250). The 20% bracket over $200,000 applies from 2026 per EY (the CRA payroll table still lists 19% as the top rate; backlog). CRA says no statutory indexing applies for 2026 (the budget raised thresholds by hand); modeled as indexed as a planning assumption.
- **NL**: The basic personal amount is $11,188 for January to June and $15,000 from July 1, 2026; $13,094 is the time-weighted 2026 amount (backlog: not read on an NL Finance page). Modeled as indexed from $13,094; from 2027 the full-year amount is $15,000 (not modeled).
