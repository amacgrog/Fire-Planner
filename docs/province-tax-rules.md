## Notes

- **ON**: Surtax: 20% of Ontario tax over $5,818 plus 36% of Ontario tax over $7,446 (both apply above the second threshold, a 56% surtax on the top slice), computed on the tax after non-refundable credits and before the dividend tax credit (the order on CRA form ON428). Until 7K the code used 16% for the second tier, a bug. The Ontario Health Premium (up to $900) is based on taxable income. Ontario tax reduction is a low-income item (not yet modeled).
- **BC**: The lowest rate is 5.60% for 2026 (BC Ministry of Finance); CRA payroll tables still show 5.06%, a stale payroll figure. No Medical Services Plan premium since 2020.
- **SK**: The senior supplement ($2,569, age 65+, claimable even when the age amount is nil) is added to the age amount and is not reduced by income. Saskatchewan raises its personal amounts by a further $500 a year by statute; modeled as ordinary indexing only.
- **MB**: The basic personal amount is $15,780 up to $200,000 of net income, reduced proportionally to nil at $400,000 (amount x (400,000 - income) / 200,000 between); it, the phase-down range and the brackets are frozen, not indexed (CRA T4127). The age amount is also unindexed for 2026 per TaxTips (factor 1.000, secondary).
- **QC**: Quebec files its own provincial return and residents get a 16.5% federal abatement (applied after the federal credits). Its age ($3,986), retirement-income ($3,541) and living-alone ($2,172) amounts are one pool, reduced by 18.75% of family income over $42,955 (Revenu Quebec form TP-1015.3-V 2026, Work Chart 2, and the Finance Quebec parameters PDF); the retirement-income amount is the lesser of $3,541 and 1.25 times the qualifying retirement income (pension and RRIF income, not CPP, QPP or OAS); for a couple the two spouses amounts are added and reduced once, by 18.75% of their combined net income over $42,955; every amount is indexed (Quebec uses its own CPI factor, 2.05% for 2026; the planner applies its plan inflation). Contributions: QPP 6.30% (basic plus first additional) on earnings from $3,500 to $74,600 and a 4% second tier to $85,000; QPIP 0.430% up to $103,000, a separate ceiling (relayed from a review citing the Act respecting the Quebec Pension Plan, CQLR c. R-9, ss. 44 to 44.3 and 50, and the Act respecting parental insurance, CQLR c. A-29.011, s. 6, which leaves the rate to a regulation; not read on a primary page: backlog). Not modeled: a spouse unused-amount transfer beyond the pooled split (each spouse takes a share of the reduced pool in proportion to their own amounts), the additional amount for a person living alone in a single-parent family (it needs a child 18 or over in post-secondary studies and no Family Allowance), the solidarity credit and work premium (paid benefits, 7L). Modeled since 7N: the Health Services Fund contribution (Revenu Quebec Schedule F: 1% of income other than employment income and OAS over 18,500, to 150 at 33,500, flat to 64,355, then 1% to a 1,000 maximum) and the RAMQ drug insurance premium of every retired adult (Schedule K table, 2026 maximum 777.48, scaled from the 2025 table; a working partner is assumed to be on a group plan; the premium is also a medical expense).
- **NS**: The old income-tested reduction of the basic personal amount no longer applies in 2026 (CRA T4127: the maximum is available at every income).
- **PE**: The fourth threshold is $142,520 (CRA T4127 and PEI Finance; the code had $142,250). The 20% bracket over $200,000 applies from 2026 per EY (the CRA payroll table still lists 19% as the top rate; backlog). CRA says no statutory indexing applies for 2026 (the budget raised thresholds by hand); modeled as indexed as a planning assumption.
- **NL**: 2026 transitional basic personal amount: $13,094, reflecting $11,188 for January to June and $15,000 from July 1, 2026 (a time-weighted figure, not an indexed amount; backlog: not read on an NL Finance page). The full-year amount is $15,000 from 2027 (not modeled); the planner indexes $13,094 forward.
