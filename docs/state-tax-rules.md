## Notes

- **AK**: Alaska levies no individual income tax on wages, pensions, retirement withdrawals, Social Security or investment gains, so no state income tax is modeled.
- **AZ**: Flat 2.5%. The standard deduction is the federal base amount (2026: $16,100 single, $32,200 joint, $24,150 head of household). Exemptions of $2,100 per filer 65 or older and $2,300 per dependent (from memory of the statute, secondary). Social Security is exempt. Net long-term gains are 75% included (the 25% subtraction). Dependent tax credit $100 per child under 17 and $25 for other dependents (the Department of Revenue page; its high-income phase-out and the $125 increase, which the governor vetoed in May 2026, are not modeled). The U.S. and Arizona government pension subtraction ($2,500) is not modeled because the planner cannot tell public pensions from private ones. Arizona has not adopted the federal $6,000 senior deduction as far as could be confirmed.
- **AR**: Top rate 3.9% on a 2026 table of 0% to $5,599, 2% to $11,199, 3% to $15,999, 3.4% to $26,399 and 3.9% above, after a $2,470 standard deduction ($4,940 joint) (Department of Finance and Administration 2026 withholding formula, read). Only half of net long-term gains is taxed. Social Security is exempt. Each filer excludes up to $6,000 of retirement income (a pension at any age, an IRA from 59 and a half). Personal tax credits of $29 per filer, per dependent and for each filer 65 or older. Military retirement pay is exempt in Arkansas but not modeled, and the planner uses one table for single and joint returns as the state does.
- **CA**: 2025 figures (CalCPA Fast Tax Facts, December 2025; secondary). These are not 2026 figures: the FTB's 2026 schedule was not published at the time of writing, so the 2025 amounts stand for the first plan year and the plan index factor takes them from there. The 1% mental health surcharge over $1 million is its own addition and does not index (the brackets, standard deduction and credits do). Exemption credits are reduced $6 each per $2,500 of federal AGI over the threshold; the 65+ credit is $153 per filer. Not modeled: CalEITC, the Young Child Tax Credit, the senior head of household credit, the joint custody HoH credit; the renter's credit assumes a full year of renting.
- **CT**: Tax tables from Form CT-1040 TCS (Rev. 12/25, tax year 2025, primary): brackets, personal exemption (reduced $1,000 per $1,000 of AGI over $30,000 / $48,000 / $38,000), the 2% add-back, the recapture and the personal tax credit decimals; none is indexed. Retirement rules from OLR report 2025-R-0152 (Sept. 2025): Social Security is exempt under $75,000 AGI ($100,000 joint or head of household), above that no more than 25% of benefits is taxed; pension, annuity and IRA income is fully exempt under $75,000 ($100,000 joint) and phases down to nothing at $100,000 ($150,000 joint), with the IRA deduction at 100% from 2026. Property tax credit up to $300 for the home, 15% less per $10,000 of AGI over $49,500 / $70,500 / $54,500 (secondary; the vehicle tax part is not modeled). The 2026 table revisions, if any, and the CT EITC are not modeled.
- **FL**: Florida levies no individual income tax on wages, pensions, retirement withdrawals, Social Security or investment gains, so no state income tax is modeled.
- **HI**: Twelve brackets from 1.4% to 11%; the 2026 brackets equal 2025 and the standard deduction is $8,000 single, $16,000 joint, $12,000 head of household (Department of Taxation Announcement 2024-03 on Act 46, read). Scheduled by statute: the wider brackets in 2027 and 2029 and the larger deductions in 2028, 2030 and 2031 (Act 46), and from 2027 a second bracket of 2.5%, a third of 5% and a 13% top bracket above $500,000 single, $750,000 head of household and $1,000,000 joint (Act 24 of 2026, Announcement 2026-06, read). $1,144 exemption per person and dependent. Social Security and employer-funded pensions are exempt; IRA and 401(k) withdrawals are treated as taxable (the pension stream is excluded in full). The 7.25% cap on long-term gains is not modeled, so gains in the higher brackets are slightly overtaxed. The food and excise tax credits for low incomes are not modeled.
- **ID**: Flat 5.3% since 2025 (Tax Commission). Idaho starts from federal taxable income: the federal standard deduction with the 65+ add-on and, after House Bill 559 (2026, retroactive to 2025), the federal senior deduction. Social Security is exempt. The food tax credit is $155 per person (2025 amount; the 2026 amount was not found) and refundable. Idaho's retirement benefits deduction for public pensions is not modeled because the planner cannot tell public pensions from private ones, and private pensions and IRA withdrawals are fully taxed.
- **IN**: State rate 2.95% in 2026 and 2.90% from 2027 (Departmental Notice #1; further cuts depend on revenue and are left out). Social Security is exempt. Every county also levies an income tax of 0.5% to 3.0% (2026 rates in the Notice); the planner uses one statewide rate of 1.77%, the average of the county rates weighted by approximate 2020 census population, applied to the state taxable income. The renter's deduction and military and other retirement deductions are not modeled.
