## Notes

- **AL**: Rates 2% / 4% / 5% (breaks at $500 and $3,000, or $1,000 and $6,000 joint). Standard deduction by income from the Department of Revenue's 2025 chart (read): $3,000 single, $8,500 joint, $5,200 head of family, falling $25, $175 and $135 per $500 of income over $25,999 to $2,500, $5,000 and $2,500 at $35,500. Exemptions $1,500 single and $3,000 joint or head of family; $1,000 per dependent at income to $50,000, $500 to $100,000, $300 above (the Form 40A chart). Federal income tax paid is deducted in full; it replaces the standard deduction when larger (the planner computes it in one extra pass; the Net Investment Income Tax that Alabama also lets you deduct is left out). Social Security and defined-benefit pensions are exempt, IRA and 401(k) withdrawals are taxed (the planner treats its pension stream as defined-benefit); the $6,000 exclusion for retirement income at 65 and the state's senior deduction were not confirmed and are not modeled.
- **AK**: Alaska levies no individual income tax on wages, pensions, retirement withdrawals, Social Security or investment gains, so no state income tax is modeled.
- **AZ**: Flat 2.5%. The standard deduction is the federal base amount (2026: $16,100 single, $32,200 joint, $24,150 head of household). Exemptions of $2,100 per filer 65 or older and $2,300 per dependent (from memory of the statute, secondary). Social Security is exempt. Net long-term gains are 75% included (the 25% subtraction). Dependent tax credit $100 per child under 17 and $25 for other dependents (the Department of Revenue page; its high-income phase-out and the $125 increase, which the governor vetoed in May 2026, are not modeled). The U.S. and Arizona government pension subtraction ($2,500) is not modeled because the planner cannot tell public pensions from private ones. Arizona has not adopted the federal $6,000 senior deduction as far as could be confirmed.
- **AR**: Top rate 3.9% on a 2026 table of 0% to $5,599, 2% to $11,199, 3% to $15,999, 3.4% to $26,399 and 3.9% above, after a $2,470 standard deduction ($4,940 joint) (Department of Finance and Administration 2026 withholding formula, read). Only half of net long-term gains is taxed. Social Security is exempt. Each filer excludes up to $6,000 of retirement income (a pension at any age, an IRA from 59 and a half). Personal tax credits of $29 per filer, per dependent and for each filer 65 or older. Military retirement pay is exempt in Arkansas but not modeled, and the planner uses one table for single and joint returns as the state does.
- **CA**: 2025 figures (CalCPA Fast Tax Facts, December 2025; secondary). These are not 2026 figures: the FTB's 2026 schedule was not published at the time of writing, so the 2025 amounts stand for the first plan year and the plan index factor takes them from there.
